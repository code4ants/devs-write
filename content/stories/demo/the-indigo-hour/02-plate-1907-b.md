The sky over the ridge had gone from black to the deep, saturated blue the old astronomers called the indigo hour. Tomi said it was the only time of night the instruments told the truth.

They walked the old service path from the archive to the dome, past the weather mast and the abandoned plate store, with Varga a few steps behind. The path was longer than she remembered. It always was in the dark.

-- There, said Tomi, before they had even reached the eyepiece. Right where the plate says.

-- That is impossible. The plate is a hundred and nineteen years old.

-- Then the star waited a hundred and nineteen years for us to look.

Back in the catalogue room, the envelope was where she had left it, but it was no longer sealed.

~~~chat
Ilinca | left | Did either of you touch the letter?
Tomi | right | I was in the dome the whole time.
Varga | right | I never touch the post. You know that.
Ilinca | left | Then who opened it?
~~~

She unfolded the single sheet. Only one line, in her own careful hand:

> *Thank you for looking up. — I.*
{.callout}

She filed the letter in drawer 14, logged it, photographed it, and asked both of them to sign.
