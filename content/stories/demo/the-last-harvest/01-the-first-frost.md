title: The First Frost
---
By the third week of October the orchard had gone the colour of old copper, and Ilinca could smell the frost coming from a day away.

~~~map {map="map0"}

~~~

She walked the rows at dawn with her hands in her pockets, counting what was left. Too many apples still hung on the high branches, red and heavy, waiting for hands that would not come in time.

-- Grandpa, the pickers did not come.

-- They never do, child. The trees pick themselves, in the end.

-- Not fast enough.

-- Then we will be faster.

The old man leaned on his pear-branch stick and looked up at the hills, where the fog was already pooling in the hollows like milk in a saucer.

> The harvest was never won by the strongest arms, he used to say. It was won by whoever started before the sun.

Ilinca read the sky once more. High, thin clouds, combed out like wool. She tied her scarf and fetched the ladders.

*Every apple is a promise, and every promise has a date.*
