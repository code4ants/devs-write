On the last morning the fog was so thick that Ilinca could not see the barn from the porch. She followed the sound of the crows up the hill, to the one tree she had never dared to strip.

The Heartwood Tree stood alone at the top of the orchard, black and crooked against the white. Its fruit was small and gold, and it hung in clusters, glowing faintly even in the grey light.

-- You came, said Grandpa Vasile.

-- You knew I would.

-- I planted it the year I was nine. I never picked it. Not once.

-- Why not?

-- Because some things are not meant for the press, child. They are meant to be kept.

Ilinca stood a long while with her hand on the rough bark. The cold crept up through her boots, and somewhere below, Corvin's engine coughed up the valley road.
