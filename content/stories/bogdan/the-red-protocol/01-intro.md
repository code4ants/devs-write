Space. The once dark and unapproachable frontier has become, in the last 300 years, just another dimension where humanity has expanded, building homes, offices, markets and an ever-expanding web of travel routes. 

On Earth, the population has migrated, mostly against its will or desire, and lives now grouped into a few, extremely large cities. Metropolis X1 is the biggest city on the old planet, with a population of roughly 3 billion souls, split into districts, with a few very distinct areas of wealth and abundance and many districts that are poor and where daily life is hard.

People traveling off the planet can choose between several space ports built throughout the city, most of them located on the outer rings of the metropolis. While still very expensive, in the last 100 years the cost for an interplanetary flight has decreased steadily. The military department is by far the biggest client of the space-travel companies, with soldiers joining missions and flying from Earth to one of the space stations that are orbiting the planet. From here, they join the frequent space shuttles that take them where their missions take place. 

Traveling through space has become a normal day-to-day experience. A less expensive space ticket can take you from Earth to one of the thousands of biodomes on Mars. A more expensive ticket can get you on board a W2 space vessel. These newer space conquering machines are equipped with Alcubierre warp drive engines, getting you across the dark void to planets and outposts far beyond what Earth's scientists of some hundreds of years ago could have dreamed or observed through their rudimentary space telescopes.

The W2 space vessels are the new toys of the military department. While the Earth is still a rich planet and a generous mother, new economic sectors have been developed, out of real need or very real greed. One of them requires gathering rare chemical materials from very distant planets. The fact that these planets are inhabited by various life forms was just a small report line on the Downsides page of a research department. That report line was quickly ticked and moved onto the Mitigated page when the Cleaners Brigade was assembled, about 30 years ago. 

The Cleaners Brigade has two main activities: killing anything that is standing in their way and gathering all the chemical materials that they can find.

The large cave gives light no chance to enter its vast, dark opening. Inside, amost no light disturbs the black air that fills this area, 300 meters below the surface of this lonely planet. The only source of luminescence are the colored phosphorescent crystals protruding from the walls. The very shy rays that these crystals emit are reflected only by the frightened eyes of the eight soldiers that are blindly trying to find a way out of this sudden underground prison. They have reached the panic level where they are mindlessly shouting, stumbling into one another and banging on the sharp rocky walls. 

A long and slick movement on the ceiling goes unnoticed by the eight unfortunate captives. One by one, the shouts and frightened movement of the soldiers are suppressed, each with a short and pained cry that echoes throughout the cave.
