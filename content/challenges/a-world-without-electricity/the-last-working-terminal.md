On the ninth day, Ilse finally stopped pressing the light switch when she walked into a room.

The village had learned quickly. Water came from the well again, bread from the communal oven, and news from whoever had last walked in from the next valley. What nobody had learned yet was how to stop expecting the old world to come back on.

> Day 9. Still nothing. I keep a list of things that used to be easy.
{.callout .red}

In the cellar of the old school, behind a wall of stacked chairs, Ilse found the hand-cranked emergency terminal the caretaker had once shown her as a joke. A small screen, a crank, and a battery of the kind nobody trusted.

~~~terminal {title="relay-crank-01"}
$ ./boot --manual
cranking... 40 turns
boot: ok
$ ping valley-east
reply from valley-east: time=412ms
~~~

She cranked until her arm shook, and a line of text appeared on the screen, then another.

~~~chat {title="Valley link"}
Tomas | left | Is anyone there? Anyone at all?
Ilse | right | Ilse, from the school in Wenn. We have a working terminal. Barely.
Tomas | left | We have a doctor and no medicine. Do you have anything?
Ilse | right | We have a well, an oven and forty minutes of crank. Let's start with that.
~~~

---

Forty minutes later the screen went dark for the last time. Ilse sat in the cellar for a while, listening to the quiet, and then climbed the stairs to tell the village that the valley was still out there.

> Day 9. The list of things that used to be easy is shorter than I thought.
{.callout .green}
