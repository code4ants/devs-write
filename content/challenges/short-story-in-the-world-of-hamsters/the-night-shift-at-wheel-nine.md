Nobody in Burrow Under-Oak liked the night shift at Wheel Nine, and Pip liked it least of all. The wheel powered the lamps of the whole lower market, which meant that when Pip got tired, the market went dark, and when the market went dark, the seed merchants got loud.

"Twelve hundred turns before dawn," said the foreman, tapping a clipboard made from a dried leaf. "Not one less."

~~~chat {title="Wheel Nine, shift log"}
Foreman Bram | left | Lamps are dimming. Is that you, Pip?
Pip | right | It's the wheel. Someone greased the left axle with sunflower oil again.
Foreman Bram | left | Sunflower oil is for eating.
Pip | right | Tell that to whoever ate half of it and left the rest on the axle.
~~~

By the ninth hundred, Pip's legs had stopped being legs and become a rumour. The lamps held. Somewhere above the burrow a shadow crossed the ventilation shaft, long and soft and patient.

> The cat is not a monster. The cat is weather. You do not fight weather: you build a good roof.
{.callout .blue}

---

At the thousandth turn the lights flickered, and every hamster in the lower market went quiet at once. Pip kept running. A small paw reached through the grille and set a single sunflower seed on the rim of the wheel, and then another, until the wheel carried a necklace of seeds around and around.

~~~

When the sun finally lit the shaft, Pip climbed down from the wheel, took the seed from the rim and, for the first time that week, ate something that had not been greased.
